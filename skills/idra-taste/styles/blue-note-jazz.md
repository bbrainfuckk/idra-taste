# Blue Note jazz covers (1956 to 1967)

**Where it comes from.** Record covers designed by Reid Miles with photographs by Francis Wolff for Blue Note Records. Type as rhythm, photos tinted in one colour and cropped hard, and the square as a stage.

**Medium.** Offset on a 12-inch square sleeve. Often 2 colours: black plus one spot, with the photograph as a duotone or monotone.

## Palette
One spot colour carries the cover.

| Spot | Hex | With |
|---|---|---|
| Blue Note blue | `#0074a2` | black, white |
| Warm yellow | `#ffb511` | black |
| Orange | `#ff6f4c` | black, cream |
| Green | `#00a95c` | black |
| Red | `#f15060` | black |

Photographs: monotone in the spot colour or black, high contrast, deep shadows.

## Type
- Condensed and heavy grotesques, set huge, tight, and cropped by the edge. Open choices: **Archivo Black**, **Archivo Narrow**, **Big Shoulders Display**, **Oswald** only if nothing else fits (overused).
- A typewriter or light sans for track lists and credits on the back.
- Words broken across lines, letters at different weights in one name, type that echoes the beat.

## Composition
- Asymmetric. Heavy type on one side, a cropped photo filling the rest.
- Extreme crops: half a face, a hand on keys, a horn bell.
- Bands and blocks of colour as structure. Rule: one big move, one counter move.

## Texture
- Clean print, slight paper warmth, halftone visible in the photo at close range.
- No grain overlay on the type.

## Fake tells
- Retro "jazz" clichés: saxophone silhouettes, music notes, piano keys as decoration.
- Full-colour photos. Use a duotone.
- Centred, symmetrical layouts.
- Thin elegant fonts. These covers were loud.

## Works for
Album and playlist covers, event series, coffee and spirits labels, editorial features, brand campaigns that need confidence, social carousels.
