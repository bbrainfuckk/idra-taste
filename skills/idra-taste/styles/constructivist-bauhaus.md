# Constructivist and Bauhaus (1919 to 1933)

**Where it comes from.** Russian constructivist posters and books, and the Bauhaus in Weimar and Dessau. Diagonals, photomontage, primary geometry, and type built from rules and bars. Study for principles: El Lissitzky, Alexander Rodchenko, the Stenberg brothers, Herbert Bayer, Joost Schmidt, Laszlo Moholy-Nagy.

**Medium.** Letterpress and offset, 2 or 3 colours (red, black, paper), photomontage from cut prints.

## Palette
| Colour | Hex | Role |
|---|---|---|
| Red | `#e43d30` | the active force |
| Black | `#141414` | structure, type |
| Paper | `#efe6d2` | ground |
| Optional | yellow `#f2c230`, blue `#2a4da0` | Bauhaus primaries, sparing |

## Type
- Geometric and grotesque sans, set in bars and blocks, often on a diagonal. Open choices: **Jost** (Futura-like), **League Gothic**, **Archivo Black**.
- Type and rules are one system: thick bars, arrows, circles carry words.
- Cyrillic or German headlines only where real and correct for the content.

## Composition
- The diagonal is the engine. Tilt the main axis 15 to 30 degrees. Everything else aligns to it or to the vertical.
- Photomontage: cut-out figures at strong scale contrast, with megaphone-like cones and circles.
- Asymmetric balance, big red shape against black type.

## Texture
- Letterpress impression: ink squash at edges, slight unevenness.
- Halftone photomontage at coarse screen, one colour.

## Fake tells
- Political iconography used as decoration (hammer and sickle, propaganda slogans) without meaning or consent. Keep the formal language, leave the symbols out unless the subject truly calls for them.
- Diagonals at random angles. One axis, held.
- Too many colours, too much ornament.

## Works for
Posters, event identities, tech and architecture brands, book covers, editorial openers, campaigns that need force and clarity.
