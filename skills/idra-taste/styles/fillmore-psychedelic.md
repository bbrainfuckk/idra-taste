# San Francisco psychedelic poster (1966 to 1969)

**Where it comes from.** Concert posters for the Fillmore and the Avalon Ballroom. Lettering swelled to fill shapes, Art Nouveau borders were borrowed from Mucha and the Vienna Secession, and colours were chosen to vibrate against each other. Study for principles: Wes Wilson, Victor Moscoso, Bonnie MacLean, Rick Griffin, Stanley Mouse and Alton Kelley.

**Medium.** Offset lithography in 2 to 4 flat inks, often with split fountain. Hand-drawn lettering, no typesetting for the headline.

## Palette
The engine is **vibration**: two complementary colours at the same lightness, so the edge between them shimmers.

| Pair | Hex | Why it works |
|---|---|---|
| Red on green | `#ff4c65` on `#44d62c` | equal value, opposite hue |
| Orange on blue | `#ff6c2f` on `#3255a4` | Moscoso's favourite clash |
| Magenta on green | `#ff48b0` on `#00a95c` | strongest flicker |
| Violet on yellow | `#9d7ad2` on `#ffe800` | softer, still vibrates |

Check a pair in grayscale: if the two colours become the same grey, the vibration will work. Keep one dark ink (`#435060` Midnight or black) for small text only.

## Type
- No font does this out of the box. Start from a fat display face (**Shrikhand**, **Bagel Fat One**, both OFL on Google Fonts), then make the letters fill a shape: convert to paths, or warp with an SVG `feDisplacementMap`, or set each line on its own scale so words swell to the silhouette.
- Letters touch and lock together. Counters shrink. Legibility is a game the reader plays.
- Small information (date, venue, ticket outlets) in a plainer line along the bottom edge, still hand-drawn in spirit.

## Composition
- One silhouette (a woman's profile, a skull, a flame, a hand) and the lettering pours into it.
- Ornamental border: whiplash curves, peacock feathers, vines. Corners are decorated.
- Strong vertical format, poster proportions near 14 x 22 inches (about 1 : 1.57).

## Texture and print
- Clean flat ink with slight misregistration between colours.
- A little paper tone. Less grain than riso, these were offset prints.
- Optional: one split-fountain band across the top or behind the lettering.

## Fake tells
- Generic "groovy" fonts used straight from the box with no shaping.
- Rainbow gradients everywhere. The originals used 2 to 4 inks.
- Tie-dye textures. That is a T-shirt, not a poster.
- Perfect readability. If the headline reads in under a second, it is too tame.

## Works for
Gig posters, festival lineups, album covers, beer and coffee labels, event identities, merch.
