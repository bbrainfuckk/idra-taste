# Polish poster school (1950s to 1980s)

**Where it comes from.** Film, theatre and circus posters in Poland, made by painters and illustrators who were free to interpret the film rather than sell it. One metaphor, painted by hand, with hand-drawn lettering. Study for principles: Henryk Tomaszewski, Jan Lenica, Franciszek Starowieyski, Roman Cieslewicz, Waldemar Swierzy, Wiktor Gorka.

**Medium.** Offset from painted originals: gouache, ink, collage. Often 3 to 5 colours, sometimes full colour from paint.

## Palette
Painterly and limited. Colour comes from the idea, not from a brand sheet.

| Mood | Colours |
|---|---|
| Circus | red `#e04a3a`, yellow `#f2c230`, black, paper `#f1ead8` |
| Drama | deep green `#2e4a3a`, flesh `#e8b89a`, black, blood red `#9e2a2a` |
| Comedy | sky `#7bb6e0`, orange `#f08a3c`, white, black |

## Type
- Hand-lettered titles that belong to the image: painted with the same brush, or cut from paper.
- No typesetting for the title. Small credits may be set plainly.
- For digital work: draw lettering as SVG paths with a brush-like wobble, or ink by hand and scan.

## Composition
- One idea. A single visual metaphor that says what the film or play is about: a face made of a map, a hat that is a bird, a hand that is a city.
- Large figure, simple ground, the title woven into the picture.
- Surreal but legible. A child understands the image, an adult understands the joke.

## Texture
- Visible paint: brush strokes, dry brush, flat gouache with soft edges.
- Paper tone and slight scan softness.

## Fake tells
- Several ideas competing. The school is about one.
- Typeset titles in a trendy font.
- Photoreal rendering. Paint and paper are the language.
- Generic surrealism (melting clocks, floating eyes) not tied to the subject.

## Works for
Film and theatre posters, book covers, editorial illustration, cultural campaigns, festival identities, album covers that need a concept.
