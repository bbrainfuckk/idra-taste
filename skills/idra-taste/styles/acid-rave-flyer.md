# Acid house and rave flyer (1988 to 1996)

**Where it comes from.** Flyers for warehouse parties and clubs in the UK, Europe and the US. Made fast and cheap, often on early Macs (Photoshop 2, Illustrator 88), scanned objects, and print shops running fluorescent inks. Study for principles: The Designers Republic (Sheffield) and archived club flyers from the period.

**Medium.** Small format (A5, A6, DL), double sided, offset or cheap litho, often with one fluorescent special ink. Front is the image, back is dense information.

## Palette
Fluorescent specials over process colour, or two fluorescents on black.

| Ink | Hex | Role |
|---|---|---|
| Fluorescent Yellow | `#ffe900` | smiley, headline |
| Fluorescent Green | `#44d62c` | acid, the 303 colour |
| Fluorescent Pink | `#ff48b0` | second special |
| Fluorescent Orange | `#ff7477` | warm special |
| Black | `#000000` | ground for the night side |
| Process cyan / magenta | `#00a0e9` / `#e4007f` | photos and fills |

## Type
- Early digital: bitmap and low-resolution outlines, extended and condensed techno sans, chrome or bevel effects done with crude early filters (one hard bevel, not a soft 3D render).
- Open fonts that fit: **Departure Mono** (pixel), **DotGothic16** for bitmap texture, **Archivo Black** and **Archivo Narrow** stretched with `font-stretch` or transform for the extended look.
- Back of flyer: a wall of information. Line-up, times, phone line ("info line"), address withheld until the night, price, "no dress code".

## Composition
- Front: one big symbol (smiley, fractal, eye, chrome logo) or a scanned object at huge scale.
- Back: centred stacked list of DJ names in descending size, logos of sound systems in a row.
- Edges crowded. Bleed everywhere. Things hit the trim.

## Texture and print
- Visible pixels on purpose: scans at low resolution, upscaled with nearest neighbour.
- Fractal or plasma backgrounds rendered at low res and posterised to 4 to 8 colours (`IdraPrint.dither` with an ink palette).
- Moire from scanned halftones, slight blur from cheap litho.

## Fake tells
- Clean synthwave sunsets and chrome grids. That is 2016 retro, not 1990.
- 3D renders with soft studio light. Early filters were harsh and obvious.
- Missing back-of-flyer information. The info density is half the look.
- Purple-to-blue gradients. Specials were yellow, green, pink.

## Works for
Club and party flyers, music releases, DJ identities, streetwear graphics, event social posts, tech brands that want sub-culture energy.
