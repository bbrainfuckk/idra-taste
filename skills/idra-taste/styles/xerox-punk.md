# Xerox punk (1976 to 1985)

**Where it comes from.** Punk fanzines, gig flyers and record sleeves made with photocopiers, scissors, glue, letraset and typewriters. Fast, cheap, angry, and deliberately raw. Study for principles: Jamie Reid, Raymond Pettibon, Linder Sterling, Gee Vaucher, and fanzines like Sniffin' Glue.

**Medium.** Black toner on white or coloured copy paper. Sometimes one extra spot colour added by screen print or a second pass.

## Palette
| Colour | Hex | Role |
|---|---|---|
| Toner black | `#111111` | everything |
| Copy paper | `#f4f2ec` | ground |
| Optional spot | Fluorescent Yellow `#ffe900`, Bright Red `#f15060` or Fluorescent Pink `#ff48b0` | one block, one word |

## Type
- Ransom-note letters cut from different sources, typewriter text, stencil letters, hand scrawl with a marker.
- Open fonts that help: **Special Elite** (typewriter), **Courier Prime**, **Stardos Stencil**, plus hand-drawn SVG.
- Mixed cases and sizes in one word is allowed here. It is the one style where that is the rule.

## Composition
- Collage: photos cut out roughly with visible edges, tape, staples.
- Blocks of text at angles, but angles with a reason (glued fast, not randomised).
- Strong black areas. Big shapes.

## Texture and print
- High contrast: photos thresholded or crushed to 1-bit with coarse halftone (`IdraPrint.halftone` at low lpi or `IdraPrint.dither` with a 2-colour palette).
- Toner noise: speckle in white areas, uneven black fill, grey smears near edges.
- Generation loss: a copy of a copy. Fine detail disappears, edges thicken.

## Fake tells
- Every element rotated a random few degrees. Real collage has some straight parts.
- Clean vector type in a "grunge" font. Use real typewriter and cut letters.
- Coloured gradients. This is toner.
- Distress textures that do not change the shapes. Photocopy changes the shapes.

## Works for
Gig posters, zines, music releases, streetwear, protest and campaign material, editorial openers, brands that want honesty and noise.
